What this is

An extension of SONCA, where every pixel owns an inherited genome and mutation happens only at birth. Here SONCA's fixed life budget is replaced by an energy budget: cells burn energy every tick for maintenance, thinking, moving and building, and can only refill it by eating. Intelligence is whatever helps a lineage find and eat food before its budget runs out.

Each cell smells every food particle through its diffusing attractant and weighs them with a small attention head made of 44 inherited weights, the same size as a SONCA agent. The Attention tab shows one cell thinking.

    What it attends to

    How a cell attends to food

    Food particles are the tokens. Each releases attractant from its surface, which spreads around walls and decays, so a cell at geodesic distance d senses about A·e−d/λ. The cell's own state forms a query, each food's chemistry forms a key, and both pass through the cell's inherited weights:

    αj = softmaxj β [ ½ q·kj + ½ (ln Aj − dj/λ − ln c0) + κ ln(1 − dj/R) ]
    q = WQ [hunger, quorum, motile, intake drop, 1]
    kj = WK [sugar, protein, prey, richness, crowding, 1]

    The distance term is a linear bias on the logits, like ALiBi in language models, but here it is simply the physics of diffusion. R is how far the cell can travel on its current energy, so unaffordable food is discounted (the energy-budget rule). Cognition widens λ and sharpens β but costs maintenance every tick. A null token, weighted by the exploration gene, lets a cell ignore faint signals and search instead. The attention-weighted gradient becomes the direction the cell grows or moves.

    Environment dials

    Rules this world never breaks

    1. Energy is conserved. Every unit enters as food and leaves as heat, structure or spores. The ledger in the corner checks the books every frame.
    2. Sensing is local. Cells only feel attractant concentration where they are; scent flows around walls, not through them.
    3. Uptake saturates. Michaelis–Menten kinetics from the food a cell sits on or touches.
    4. Division needs reserves. A cell divides only once it can pay for a daughter's structure, the biosynthetic heat, and a reserve for both.
    5. Mutation happens only at birth, as in SONCA. Genomes are otherwise inherited exactly.
    6. Brains are expensive. Maintenance rises with cognition (the expensive-tissue trade-off).
    7. Moving costs energy and motile cells pay a higher upkeep.
    8. Public goods need a quorum. Digestive enzyme is secreted only when enough kin signal nearby, and it works only at the prey's surface.
    9. Sharing is between kin, through junctions both cells must build, with transfer losses.
    10. Starving cells go dormant. Spores cost little to keep, travel on fruiting bodies, and wake when food is sensed.
    11. One cell per site. Hard prey is impenetrable until digested; the dead are recycled as nutrient.
